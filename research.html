RESEARCH TOPIC / 01

Machine Learning

I use machine learning to explore nonlinear relationships in structural engineering data. The focus is on comparing predictive methods and interpreting how the inputs influence the response, from concrete strength to earthquake intensity.

Research focus

Regression and ensemble methods offer ways to study relationships that are difficult to capture with a single simplified equation. My published work examines concrete compressive strength and hybrid ensemble prediction of peak ground acceleration.

RESEARCH TOPIC / 02

Multi Hazard Resilience

My interests in resilience connect the behavior of individual buildings with decisions about safety after damage. Current work considers fire-damaged reinforced-concrete buildings and ground-motion modeling in highly seismic regions.

ONGOING RESEARCH

Multi-hazard assessment of fire-damaged buildings

Assessing the seismic capacity of reinforced-concrete residential buildings in Kathmandu that sustained extensive fire damage during the 2025 unrest and were subsequently reoccupied without structural retrofitting.

In collaboration with Dr. Prashidha Khatiwada (University of Melbourne) and Rajeendra Godakandage (RMIT University).

Context-image references: NDTV · The Wire.

ONGOING RESEARCH

Component attenuation models for highly seismic regions

Exploring calibration of component attenuation models for high-seismicity regions such as the Hindu Kush. These models were originally developed primarily for low-to-moderate seismicity settings.

In collaboration with Dr. Nelson Lam and Dr. Prashidha Khatiwada (University of Melbourne).

The paper below addresses ground-motion prediction, a related input to seismic hazard assessment; it does not report results from the ongoing fire-damage study.

RESEARCH TOPIC / 03

System Identification and Structural Health Monitoring

I study how measured response can reveal a structure’s dynamic properties and help test the assumptions in numerical models. System identification and modal testing provide a basis for comparing observed behavior with predictions and informing structural health monitoring.

ONGOING MASTER’S RESEARCH

Measured building response and model updating

Using ambient vibration and earthquake measurements to identify dynamic properties and examine finite-element model discrepancies for a reinforced-concrete building in Bangkok. This work is supervised by Prof. Pennung Warnitchai at the Asian Institute of Technology.

MASTER’S TERM PROJECT

Dynamic characterization of a three-story steel frame

Combined analytical eigenvalue modeling, experimental modal analysis, and output-only operational modal analysis of a three-degree-of-freedom frame. Methods included impact-hammer and shake-table testing, frequency response functions, Frequency Domain Decomposition, and Stochastic Subspace Identification.

Python and MATLAB supported signal processing and comparisons between analytical and measured response. The project examined how added mass and modeling assumptions affect modal frequencies and shapes.

RESEARCH TOPIC / 04

Computational Modeling and Efficiency

I work with finite-element modeling and optimization to explore efficient structural designs. This includes studying the trade-offs between structural weight, response constraints, and computational effort.

MASTER’S TERM PROJECT

Metaheuristic optimization of a 3D steel truss

Developed a vectorized direct-stiffness solver in Python and coupled it to a modified particle swarm optimizer. The design problem considered steel weight under stress, displacement, and buckling constraints across gravity, lateral, and combined load cases.

The implementation explored adaptive inertia, penalties for infeasible designs, and recovery from stagnation while handling unstable structural configurations.
