ONGOING

Listening to tall buildings: measured vibration and finite-element model updating

How accurately do conventional numerical models reproduce the behavior of a tall building that is already standing? This master's research uses ambient vibration and earthquake measurements to identify dynamic properties, examine model discrepancies, and test sensitivity-based updating for a reinforced-concrete building in Bangkok.

ONGOING

From individual building vulnerability to a community-scale picture

An ongoing exploration of earthquake and multi-hazard vulnerability of reinforced-concrete buildings, including nonlinear response, fragility assessment, and questions of how building-level information might inform community-scale assessment. Methods and findings are still being developed.

PUBLISHED

Optimization of Truss Structures Using Nature-Inspired Algorithms with Frequency and Stress Constraints

Comparing Red Kite and Secretary Bird optimization algorithms to reduce truss weight while satisfying stress and natural-frequency limits. The study evaluates performance across five benchmark trusses, including convergence, computational cost, and consistency across independent runs.

PUBLISHED

Machine learning and concrete compressive strength

Using a dataset of 1,206 concrete test results, this work examined nonlinear relationships between concrete mix inputs and compressive strength. Multiple machine-learning approaches were compared, with sensitivity analysis used to interpret the influence of material and mix variables.

Project details ↗

PUBLISHED

Hybrid ensemble learning for peak ground acceleration prediction

This publication investigates nonlinear machine-learning models and a hybrid stacked ensemble to predict peak ground acceleration. It tests the model against regional earthquake cases and explores the role of the input variables in the predictions.
